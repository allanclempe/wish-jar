🫙 Wish Jar Privacy Policy

Last updated 19 October 2026

Wish Jar is an open-source project that helps kids earn their wishes by completing daily tasks. It runs as a phone app, on the web at wish-jar.blastin.dev, on photo frames, and on Echo Show devices through the Wish Jar Alexa skill. This policy says what Wish Jar keeps, why, and how to remove it. It covers all of these.

The short version

  • There's no sign-in, account or email address. Wish Jar never asks who you are.
  • What you put in Wish Jar is kept in your family's own jar. Only the devices you've joined to your family can read it.
  • There's no advertising and no tracking, and nothing is sold or shared for marketing.
  • Unused families are deleted automatically, with their photos.
  • The code is public, so you can check all of this yourself: github.com/allanclempe/wish-jar.

What's in a family's jar

A parent sets up the jar. It holds:

  • each kid's first name (or a nickname) and an emoji or, only if a parent chooses to add one, a photo;
  • the tasks, wishes, coins, bonus coins and consequences the family sets up, and which tasks were ticked off on which day;
  • the list of the family's devices, and when each was last seen;
  • a parent PIN, if one is set, to keep grown-up settings away from kids.

A copy of the jar is also kept on each of the family's devices, so it keeps working without a connection.

On the Echo Show

The Echo Show forgets everything at the end of each skill session. So that it can reopen your family's jar, the Wish Jar skill gives the page your Echo Show's Alexa device ID and a random secret kept for that Echo Show. Wish Jar keeps only a one-way hash of each, which can't be turned back into the ID. Wish Jar doesn't receive your name, voice recordings, location, contacts or Amazon account details. Amazon's own handling of your Alexa use is covered by Amazon's privacy notice.

What Wish Jar doesn't collect

No names or email addresses of grown-ups, no phone numbers, no location, no contacts, no advertising IDs, and no analytics or tracking scripts. Kids' details are only what a parent chooses to type in, and a photo is never required.

Where it's kept, and who handles it

Family jars and photos are stored with Cloudflare, which runs Wish Jar's servers. Like any website, Cloudflare sees the IP address of each request to deliver it. Wish Jar uses it only to limit how often one address can start families or ask for join codes, keeps those counts for at most an hour, and keeps short-lived error logs for a few days to fix problems. Starting a family uses Cloudflare Turnstile, a check that a person (not a script) is starting it.

A family's data is sent over encrypted connections (HTTPS), and each device gets its own token. Without one, the jar can't be read.

Children

Wish Jar is made for families, and kids use it to tick off their tasks. A parent or guardian sets up the family and decides what goes in it. Wish Jar doesn't ask kids for personal information, doesn't show ads, and doesn't let kids contact anyone. A parent can change or delete anything about their kids at any time.

Deleting your data

  • A kid or a photo: edit the kid and change or remove the photo, or choose "Delete kid". Their photo is deleted with them.
  • A device: remove it under Devices. It loses access to the jar straight away.
  • The whole family: a family is deleted automatically, with all its photos, once none of its devices has been used for 6 months, or 7 days after it was started if it was never used again. To have your family deleted sooner, ask us (below).

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version is posted here with a new date. Every change is also visible in the project's public history on GitHub.

Contact

Questions or requests, including deleting your family's jar: open an issue at github.com/allanclempe/wish-jar/issues. Please don't post your family's details or a join code there.
